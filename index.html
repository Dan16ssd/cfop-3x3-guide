Solve the cube in four stages.

CFOP is the method most world-class solvers use. You build a white cross, pair up and insert the first two layers, then finish the yellow layer with memorized algorithms. This guide starts with the 2-look last layer (10 OLL and 6 PLL algorithms), then goes on to COLL and ZBLL for sub-10 and sub-8 solving.

Notation

Hold the cube with white on the bottom and green in front. Every diagram on this page uses that orientation.

Each letter is a face. A plain letter turns that face 90° clockwise, as if you were looking straight at that face. An apostrophe (prime) means counter-clockwise. A 2 means a half turn.

MoveLayerWhat it means
R / R'RightRight face clockwise: the front of the right layer goes up. R' brings it down.
L / L'LeftLeft face clockwise: the front of the left layer goes down.
U / U'UpTop layer clockwise from above: the front pieces move to the left.
D / D'DownBottom layer clockwise from below: the front pieces move to the right.
F / F'FrontFront face clockwise: the top of the front layer moves right.
B / B'BackBack face clockwise, as seen from behind.
MMiddle sliceThe slice between L and R, turned the same direction as L.
r / fWide movesTurn the face and the middle slice next to it together. Also written Rw, Fw.
x y zWhole cubeRotate the whole cube like R, U and F.
U2Half turnTurn twice. Direction doesn't matter for the result.

Stage 1

Cross

Put the four white edges around the white center so each edge's side color also matches its center. Solve it on the bottom from the start: flipping the cube after the cross wastes time and hides your F2L pieces.

A cross can always be solved in 8 moves or fewer. Use the 15-second inspection to plan the whole cross before you start turning.

Beginner path

  1. Solve the cross on top first (the "daisy" method is fine for a week).
  2. Then switch to solving it on top but planning it.
  3. Then solve it on the bottom, looking at the white stickers from the sides.

Plan in inspection

  • Find all 4 white edges.
  • Pick 2 that are easy to place relative to each other, then add the rest.
  • Say the moves in your head before you start.

Common cases

  • Edge in the bottom layer but flipped: take it out with one turn of its face, then insert it again from the side.
  • Edge in the middle layer: one turn (R, R', L, L') drops it in, but check you don't knock out a solved edge.
  • Edge on top, white facing up: U moves to line it up, then a half turn of that face.
Stage 2

F2L · First two layers

Each bottom corner goes into its slot together with the middle edge above it. Join them in the top layer, then insert the pair in one motion. Learn F2L intuitively: understand why each case works, so you can mirror it to any slot.

All cases below target the front-right slot (green and orange). Colored pieces are the pair and the cross; gray pieces don't matter. Front-left slot? Mirror the moves with L and F, or use U' F' U F style inserts to avoid rotating the cube.

Advanced

Advanced F2L

Advanced F2L is what separates a 15-second solver from a sub-10 one. It has two parts: knowing a short, rotation-free algorithm for every F2L case in every slot, and the techniques below that save moves and pauses between pairs.

X-cross and XX-cross

Solve the cross and the first pair (X-cross) or two pairs (XX-cross) together, planned in the 15-second inspection. A good X-cross saves 5–8 moves. The solver below always tries cross, X-cross and XX-cross and keeps the shortest.

Keyhole

When a slot is still empty, use it as a free spot: drop a corner or edge in through the empty slot without breaking anything. Great for corners stuck in the bottom layer.

Multi-slotting

While inserting one pair, set up the next: choose the insert (for example R U R' vs R U' R') that also pairs or frees the next pieces.

Pseudo-slotting

Insert a pair into the wrong slot on purpose with the D layer turned, then fix the D layer later. Common in X-cross planning.

Back slots without rotations

Insert back pairs with R' U R, L U' L' or B moves instead of y rotations. Every case below has an algorithm for each slot.

Edge control on the last pair

Insert the last pair so all four yellow edges end up oriented (ZBLS / VHLS). Then the last layer is one ZBLL or a COLL plus edge PLL.

Full set

Full OLL · 57 algorithms

Full OLL makes the top yellow in one look instead of two. Learn it after you're comfortable with 2-look and full PLL. A good order: the 7 cross cases (already done if you know 2-look), then the shapes you meet most often in your solves.

Cases are grouped by the shape the yellow stickers make. Numbers follow the standard OLL numbering. Some algorithms end with the top layer turned, so do a final U turn if needed (PLL comes next anyway).

Full set

Full PLL · 21 algorithms

Full PLL solves the top layer's positions in one look. It's usually the first full set people learn, because it's only 21 algorithms and saves about 2–3 seconds over 2-look. Arrows show where each piece goes.

Recognize from two sides: look for headlights (matching corner colors on one side), blocks of 2 or 3 matching stickers, and whether opposite colors sit next to each other.

Advanced

COLL · Corners of the last layer

COLL is used when the yellow cross is already on top but the corners aren't oriented. One algorithm orients and places the 4 corners, so the last step is always an edges-only PLL (Ua, Ub, H or Z), or the cube is just solved. That replaces two looks (corner OLL + corner PLL) with one.

There are 42 cases: 6 for each corner shape (Sune, Antisune, Pi, U, T, L), 4 for H, and 2 where the corners are already oriented and only need swapping. Recognize the shape first, then look at the side colors of the corners.

Diagrams show the corners' side colors. Edges are shown gray because COLL doesn't care where they are.

Each algorithm below combines short verified algorithms (Sune, Niklas, Headlights, Bowtie…) and was checked in a cube simulator, so every one is correct. Popular COLL sheets sometimes use faster, more specialized algorithms for some cases. Once you know a case, feel free to swap in one that suits your hands. "⁻¹" means do that algorithm backwards (inverse).
Expert

ZBLL · The whole last layer in one look

ZBLL solves the entire last layer with one algorithm when the yellow cross is already formed. It is what most sub-8 solvers work toward. It has 493 cases, so nobody learns it in one go. Solvers add it set by set over months or years.

ZBLL only works if all 4 yellow edges are already oriented when you reach the last layer. You make that happen while inserting the last F2L pair, with an edge-control technique such as ZBLS or VHLS. Without edge control, about 7 out of 8 solves won't have the cross ready.

Before you start ZBLL

  • Full OLL and full PLL learned and fast.
  • COLL learned (each COLL case is the base of a ZBLL set).
  • Consistently sub-10 and comfortable with edge control on the last pair.

How to recognize a case

  • Identify the COLL case (corner shape + corner colors).
  • Then look at where the edges are relative to the corners. Each COLL case splits into 12 ZBLL cases.
  • Drill recognition with the trainer before speed.

Finger tricks tutorial

Pick a move and watch the hands do it. The finger or wrist doing the turn lights up blue, the layer turns, and the hand returns to home grip. Use Slow motion to follow each finger, and Play practice to see a whole algorithm with real fingering. Drag the cube to look from behind at the middle and ring fingers.

Finger or hand doing the move T thumb · I index · M middle · R ring

Grip pressure

  • Hold just firmly enough that the cube won't fall.
  • A tight grip slows every flick and causes lockups.
  • Your thumbs carry most of the cube's weight.

Wrist vs. fingers

  • R, L and r come from the wrist.
  • U, U', D, F, M are single-finger flicks.
  • After every wrist turn, return to home grip.

Avoid

  • Cube rotations (y) in F2L when an F or U' insert works.
  • Looking at your hands.
  • Pausing between moves of an algorithm you know.

Metronome turning

One move per click through a whole solve. If you can't find the next piece in time, slow down. This trains look-ahead.

90 bpm

Start near 60–90 bpm. Add 10 when a full solve has no pauses.

Blind cross

Inspect for 15 seconds, close your eyes, solve the cross. Open your eyes and check. Forces full cross planning.

Slow solves

Half speed, zero pauses. A solver who never stops at 3 moves per second beats one who sprints at 6 and pauses.

No-rotation F2L

Solve the cross, then F2L without any y. Count rotations and bring it to zero.

Train like Yiheng Wang

Yiheng Wang is a Chinese speedcuber and 3x3 world-record holder who solves with CFOP. His training details are not published as an official program, but a few habits are on record:

Practice almost every day

In his Guinness World Records profile he says he practises cubing almost every day.

Source: Guinness World Records

Review your own solves

He watches videos of his solves to find where he can get faster.

Source: Guinness World Records

Plan, then focus on rhythm

He predicts the solution during inspection so the solve itself is about rhythm, not searching.

Source: Guinness World Records

Coached, focused volume

He trained up to six hours a day before competitions with his coach Li Chunxiao, later dropping to one or two hours a day.

Roadmap

Sub-2:00
Learn notation and solve the cube with any beginner method. Learn the cross on the bottom.
Sub-1:00
Intuitive F2L (the 12 cases here). 2-look OLL and PLL. Start finger tricks.
Sub-30
No-rotation F2L, all slots. Fluent finger tricks. Daily drills and metronome solves.
Sub-20
Full PLL (21 algorithms). Plan cross + first pair in inspection. Look-ahead with no pauses.
Sub-15
Full OLL (57), advanced F2L cases, film-and-review training like the routine above.
Sub-10
COLL (42 cases) for solves with the cross already on top. Plan an x-cross (cross plus first pair) in inspection. Turn smoothly at roughly 8–10 moves per second with almost no pauses. Use multi-slotting: set up the next pair while inserting the current one.
Sub-8
Edge control on the last pair (ZBLS/VHLS) and ZBLL subsets, starting with T and U. Plan the full x-cross every solve. Short bursts above 11 moves per second. Do daily drills, film your solves and target your slowest stage.
